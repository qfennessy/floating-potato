Venus Balloon Navigator

Balloons can't steer, but they can pick their wind. Higher is faster, lower is slower, and the north-south drift flips between them. Our agent picks its altitude to reach science targets and chase eruptions its sensors hear.

Fly it yourself 56.0 km Keys: ↑ ↓ change altitude, space pauses. High = fast and drifts poleward; low = slow and drifts toward the equator.

Altitude

Dots on the lines and ticks in the rows below mark when each balloon scored (bigger = more points; faint or hollow = didn't count because of a relay outage). The dashed line is the day-30 finish. Green band: the 50–62 km safe corridor. Below it the envelope overheats; above it the acid cloud top ends the ride. Higher means faster wind.

Commander log

    All five worlds

    Each world has its own launch point, its own weather (the height where north-south drift flips moves between 52 and 60 km), and its own random eruptions. A fixed balloon gets one altitude for every world, picked before launch. Points: fixed sites 1, lightning 1, eruption 2, gas plume 3. Every balloon carries the same sensors, so a drifter scores too if it happens to float through an event.
